# S1_Global_Inundation_Map_2015_2024
we use the Sentinel-1 archive to construct a near-global, observation-based record of inundation from 2015 to 2024. We develop a pixel-level time-series anomaly detection framework that identifies temporary inundation from deviations in radar backscatter, enabling flood occurrence to be mapped consistently across countries and years with independence of any event-specific catalog. By integrating the resulting inundation inventory with gridded population data, World Bank income classifications ((high income, H), (upper-middle income, UM), (lower-middle income, LM) and (low income, L)) and major climate zones, we quantify how the relationship between observed inundated area and population exposure varies across income groups. We find that LM income countries consistently account for a disproportionately large share of the global flood-affected population and exhibit a stronger-than-proportional increase in exposure as inundated area expands. The persistence of this pattern across multiple hydroclimatic zones indicates that the observed disparity in population exposure faced by LM income countries extends beyond climate setting and warrants greater attention in global flood-risk reduction efforts.
